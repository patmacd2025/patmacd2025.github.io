Mercury

Mercury is the closest planet to the sun and is the smallest planet in our solar system.

It is on average 58 million kilometers away from the sun and has a radius of 4,879 kilometers.

Mercury's surface temperatures are both extremely hot and cold. Because the planet is so close to the Sun, day temperatures can reach highs of 430°C, but without an atmosphere to retain that heat at night, temperatures can dip as low as -180°C.

Mercury orbits the sun every 88 earth days and travels at a speed of 47 kilometers per second which is fastes speed out of all the planets in our solar system.

One Mercury solar day (one full day-night cycle) is 176 Earth days which is just over two years on Mercury This means that a day on Mercury is nearly twice as long as a year.

Mercury's axis of rotation is tilted just 2 degrees. That means it spins nearly perfectly upright and so does not experience seasons as many other planets do.

The gravitational pull of Mercury is 3.7m/s² which is 0.38 times that of the Earth.
